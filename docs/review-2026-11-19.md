# tiny-speech: independent review, improved architecture, city scaling, build plan

Reviewer: Claude (Fable 5.2), 19 Nov 2026, asked by the owner to review the proposal. Reviewed: `tiny-speech/ARCHITECTURE.md` (draft of 18 Nov), the built
core v0 (`browser/`, `test/`, `core/`), the downloaded MapSMA /page5 and CredBrief /page40 files, tiny-voca upstream
(commit 32c5801), and the Sherpa-ONNX docs source. Nothing under `/home/user/ranked-feed` was changed.

Marks used: **[checked]** = I read it in the sources or measured it here. **[unverified]** = I could not check it here.

Short verdict: the decisions (D1–D5) are sound and the v0 core is careful work. The proposal's weak points are
(1) it rests its headline size on a recognizer nobody has trained, with no gate for giving up, (2) it never
notices that San Miguel is welded into the code it wants to "lift", and (3) the one cheap, certain win (the
voice in plain JS) is presented with less caution than it needs. The new city requirement makes (2) the first
thing to fix, and most of that work can be done and tested in this container.

---

## 1. Review, ranked by consequence

### 1.1 The city is in the code, not in the data (blocks the new requirement)

Step 4 says "lift `read.js` / `ask.js` into `domains/places`". Lifted as they are, they carry San Miguel with
them. **[checked]** in the downloaded files:

| Where | What is San Miguel-only |
|---|---|
| `read.js` `ALIASES`, `EXACT_ONLY`, `EXACT_NAME`, `NO_SHORT` | hand-kept tables keyed by place id ("60": azotea, "X1": kanbei…) inside the reader's source |
| `read.js` `parse()` | areas as regexes: `san antonio`, `guadalupe`, `aurora\|fabrica`; Centro = `jardin\|zocalo\|…`; `q.susan` (Susan York, a local blogger) is a field of the request |
| `ask.js` | `nowMX()` with `America/Mexico_City` fixed; `CENTRO_M = 650` (metres from the Jardín); `WALK_M_PER_MIN = 75` ("San Miguel's hills and cobbles"); `FAR_M = 25000` from the Jardín; `RECENT_DAYS` for Susan's picks |
| `spanish.js` | `ENGLISH` and `BY_HAND` word lists taken from this city's place names |
| `inline.js` | the Whisper hint begins `Centro, Jardín, Susan York, …` |
| core v0 workers | a `SITES` table with `mapsma` and `credbrief` and host-name regexes inside `asr-worker.js` and `tinyvoca-worker.js` |
| proposal layout | `core/es-mx.js`: a country in the name of a core file |

Change: before anything is lifted, split every one of these into (a) a city-free rule in code and (b) a row in a
city pack (section 3). The split is testable today with golden tests (plan step B0–B3).

### 1.2 The ears carry the headline number and have no gate

"Total about 20–35 MB against 100–170 MB" depends on a 5–15 MB bilingual streaming phone recognizer trained from
scratch. The proposal calls it "the research bet" but then builds the size table, the language gate ("~0") and the
"no runtime" promise on it.

- "tiny-voca in reverse" is a slogan, not a design. tiny-voca is small because its job is easy in the ways that
  matter: one synthetic speaker, clean audio, explicit durations given by an aligner. Recognition is the opposite
  case: every speaker, every phone microphone, street noise, two languages, accented English and accented Spanish
  (San Miguel has many of both). Nothing in tiny-voca's recipe says that inverts at the same size.
- The one size reference offered, Sherpa's 3M keyword spotter, is **[checked]** real (int8 encoder 4.4 MB, decoder
  743 K, joiner 85 K) but it is Chinese + English, and it spots given phrases; it is not a recognizer for questions
  like "three cheap places with jazz near the Parroquia tomorrow".
- Synthetic training speech from one teacher voice teaches a recognizer that voice. Real Mexican Spanish data
  (Common Voice "México", CIEMPIESS) exists; hours, licences and whether it covers tourists' English are **[unverified]**.
- CredBrief's main use is open questions, which "keep Whisper tiny (~40 MB) as an optional download". So for
  one of the two sites the tiny ears save nothing.
- No stop rule. Add one: the tiny recognizer ships only if, on the recorded test set, command success is not
  worse than Whisper tiny + the reader, per language, and first-partial time is better. Until then it is a
  branch, and every size table shows two columns: "measured today" and "if the ears work".

What I would do instead of waiting: make the rest of the stack tolerant of bad hearing, which is where MapSMA
already wins (sound keys, consonant skeletons, "Did you mean…?"). Fix the `AsrEngine` result shape now so any
recognizer can return more than one string: `{text, lang, langScores, alternatives?, phones?, confidence?}`. Then the
ears are a swap, not a rebuild. And state the requirement that makes the ears worth doing at all for many cities:
**a new city must need zero training** (acoustic model city-free; words and names come from the pack at load).

### 1.3 Name hints already hit the ceiling in one city

**[checked]** `asr-worker.js`: Whisper takes at most 223 prompt tokens, the page used 214 on 17 Nov, and
`inline.js` line 430 says: "our picks only: the hint has no room for more". 96 places exist; the 18 "extra" places
and most sights are already left out of the hint. Music venues (D5), band names and a second city cannot fit.
The proposal's answer ("any engine needs biasing; Sherpa: hotwords") skips this; Sherpa hotwords also need a
transducer with `modified_beam_search` and BPE units **[checked]**, i.e. a different engine.

Change: treat the hint as a small, ranked budget built per pack (and per language), not as "the names". Rank by
how hard a name is to hear without help (build-time test) and how often it is asked; put the rest through
after-the-fact sound matching, which already recovers "La Sotea" and "Tastavir". Measure what the hint really
buys per name: the "10/10 vs 8/10" figure is ten questions, too few to plan on.

### 1.4 The voice step is safer than the ears, but not as safe as written

- **Speed and memory on a phone are unmeasured.** **[checked]** upstream `js/README.md`: 9.7× real time on an Apple
  M5, one thread, Node 24; peak memory 238 MB in Node (518 MB in Bun), although it "holds" about 46 MB once
  loaded. Upstream calls the plain-JS version "an experiment". A mid-range Android phone may be 5–10× slower than
  an M5 core **[unverified]**: a two-second sentence could take over a second to start. The existing page plays each
  sentence as it is made, which helps, but step 2 must be gated on measured first-sound time and peak memory on
  Lars's iPhone and one cheap Android, with the ONNX worker kept as the fallback behind `VoiceEngine`.
- **Step 2 makes Spanish worse before it makes it better.** "Spanish answers in the same voice through rules
  (American accent until step 5)" replaces the phone's own Mexican voice (today's behaviour, **[checked]**
  `inline.js` `spanishVoice`) with whole Spanish sentences in 59 American-English phones: no trill, no ñ as one
  sound, no /x/, diphthongised e and o (`eɪ`, `oʊ`). That is acceptable for a name inside an English sentence,
  which is what `spanish.js` was written for. For a whole answer it is a step back for Spanish speakers, and step 5
  needs a GPU. Keep the device's es-MX voice as the default for Spanish answers until the bilingual voice passes a
  listening test; offer the tiny-voca Spanish as a setting.
- **"About ten Spanish phones and a language token … fine-tuned from the released checkpoint"** is plausible
  (`vocab_size` is 76 **[checked]**; the embedding table is 16,704 parameters; a Korean + English port exists) but
  **[unverified]**: every Spanish port listed upstream is a separate Castilian model, none is bilingual
  English/Spanish and none is Mexican. A Mexican teacher voice of good enough quality is also **[unverified]**.
  Treat step 5 as an experiment with its own gate (name intelligibility by Whisper, plus the owner's ear).
- **Sizes.** "One 8 MB file" is right for the JS pack (7,988,384 bytes **[checked]** via the Hugging Face header).
  The README's "18 MB" is the ONNX form. "8–10 MB, at most ~32 MB" is an odd range; say "8 MB today; a bilingual
  model is expected to stay under 10 MB (unverified)". "About 45 MB" for ONNX + Pyodide today: **[unverified]**.
- **Spanish text normalisation is missing.** dizione's `normalize.js` is 65 KB of English rules (numbers, money,
  dates, units). Spanish needs its own. For places this can be avoided by rule: the responder only emits
  speakable words (as `sayTimeEs` already does). For reading news in Spanish it cannot be avoided; say so and
  keep Spanish news reading out of scope until then.

### 1.5 Per-question language detection is under-designed

"Whisper's language guess, then the matching model" means two models in memory (multilingual tiny for the guess
and Spanish, tiny.en for English, about 40 MB each **[checked]** in the worker's comments) on the phone that
already ran out of memory with two onnxruntime instances. And the hard case is the common one here: an English
question full of Spanish names ("Is La Azotea open?").

Changes:
1. Decide by measurement whether one multilingual Whisper tiny can serve both languages (halves the download,
   removes the model switch). If English loses too much, keep two files but only one loaded at a time.
2. Make the guess two-way, not 99-way: compare only the `<|en|>` and `<|es|>` scores of one decoder step, add a
   prior (last question's language, page language), and expose the margin.
3. Check after transcription with text, which is free and deterministic: function-word evidence (the
   `core/lid/text-lid.js` draft) with place names masked out first, so "La Azotea" is not Spanish evidence. Only a
   low-margin disagreement triggers one re-run in the other language.
4. Notice that MapSMA's reader is already language-blind: one `parse()` reads English and Spanish words together
   **[checked]**. Keep it that way. Then a wrong language guess costs recognition quality and the answer's
   language, not understanding, and code-switched questions mostly work instead of being "out of scope".
5. Park `core/lid/race.js`. Two streaming recognizers at once contradict the memory lesson, and the proposal
   itself says the language head would replace it.

### 1.6 The data has two sources of truth, and music is a freshness problem

- **[checked]** `data.js` `HOURS` and `/photos/hours.json` disagree for 29 of the 54 places in `hours.json`
  (place 1: 9:00–18:00 against 8:30–17:30). The page overlays `hours.json` at run time, so a failed fetch
  silently answers from older hours. 26 non-sight places have no entry in `hours.json`; 8 have no facts.
  A pack needs one source, with `checked` dates the answer can mention.
- **[checked]** `live_music` is on three places (43, 69, X17), not two. The proposed music shape
  (`when: {"thu": "20:00–23:00"}`) uses day names where opening hours use "0"–"6" with Monday = 0: use one form.
- "Who is playing" is event data: it goes stale in days, not months. The proposal has no owner, source, expiry
  or wording for stale music data. Without those, ship only recurring slots ("jazz on Thursdays from 8") with a
  checked date, and no band names.

### 1.7 The privacy promise needs exact words and self-hosted files

"Your voice stays on your phone" is true for audio. But **[checked]**: the workers import transformers.js,
onnxruntime and Pyodide from jsdelivr and the model from huggingface.co at run time (third parties see the
visitor's address; a CDN change is a supply-chain risk; no integrity hashes), and CredBrief sends the question's
text to its server and posts client error reports. Change: serve every engine file from the site itself, pinned
by SHA-256 in a manifest; word the promise as "the recording never leaves your phone; a news question is sent to
CredBrief as text"; make "no audio in any request" a Playwright test.

### 1.8 Sizes are guessed where they could be measured

- Interpreter "1–5 MB": **[checked]** `read.js` + `ask.js` + `spanish.js` are 122 KB (40 KB gzipped); all voice-relevant
  place data (places, info, hours, facts) is 58.5 KB (14.9 KB gzipped). Off by a factor of 10 or more.
- `data.js` is 1.3 MB, but 1.25 MB of it is the map (roads, blocks, names). That matters for city packs: the
  voice needs 15 KB a city, not 1.3 MB.
- "100–170 MB off-the-shelf" mixes stacks. Today's MapSMA English path is about 40 (Whisper) + 10–20
  (onnxruntime wasm) + ~45 (voice + Pyodide) ≈ 95–105 MB. The plain-JS voice alone would bring that to about
  60 MB, with no research. That is the real near-term number; the proposal buries it.
- "Flexibility … about 8–12 MB" (full dictionary, language model, tiny classifier) has no measurement behind it.

Change: a `tools/sizes.mjs` that prints transfer sizes from the built files, and a table in the document generated
from it.

### 1.9 The "flexibility" paragraph is a list of ideas without interfaces

Full dictionaries, a weighted language model, sound-spellings, a bilingual concept table, a "tiny classifier",
LLM-written questions. Each is reasonable; together they have no order and no test. The classifier also needs a
sentence about determinism: fixed weights give the same answer every time, but they are not rules the owner can
read. Order I recommend: (1) concept tables in data, (2) an LLM-written question set used as tests and as a mine
for synonyms, reviewed as a diff, (3) a classifier only if measured misses remain.

### 1.10 Smaller points

- **No tests for the part that matters most.** The 19 tests cover the plumbing. The reader and the asker (114 KB
  of rules) have none in this repo; whether the sites have them is **[unverified]**. Refactoring them without
  golden tests would be reckless.
- **Step 0 (recorded test set) is right and is not started.** It needs people, not this container. It should
  record phone, place (street, restaurant, room) and speaker's first language, and it should be split per city.
- **Settings in shared code.** `listen.js` exports `REC_MAX_MS = 5000`; MapSMA uses its own 8000. Make limits
  per-app configuration.
- **Endpointing by loudness** is fine in a room. On a street in the Centro or inside a bar with live music
  (the D5 use case) it is **[unverified]**; Silero VAD needs onnxruntime, against the no-runtime goal. Put noisy
  recordings in the test set before deciding.
- **News.** `feed.xml` was last built 26 Sep 2026, has no item dates, and its categories are free text
  ("sports, college football") **[checked]**. "Latest" and "today" cannot be answered from it honestly. Keep the
  news domain to: search titles, read one, next, stop, and the hand-off to CredBrief.
- **Sherpa facts in section 2**: Whisper-tiny LID 98 MB (12 + 86 MB int8), `en-20M` encoder 41 MB int8, Nemotron
  encoder 627 MB: all **[checked]**. Community Spanish models, Supertonic, Piper voices: **[unverified]**.
- **Versioning.** `?v=` strings changed by hand in several files ("Change V and every ?v= below together") will
  not survive packs, engines and a core that version separately. Use one manifest with content hashes.

## 2. Improved architecture (amends sections 0, 4, 4a, 5 of ARCHITECTURE.md)

Decisions D1–D5 stand. Add one:

| # | Decision | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| D6 | **Cities are data** (19 Nov 2026) | No city, country or site name in code. A city is a pack built from data and loaded at run time. A new city needs no code change and no model training. |

### 2.1 Four layers

```
app config      one small object per site: which domains, which city or cities, UI language, limits, cache prefix
city pack       data for one city: places, areas, hours, music, names, local words, collections   (section 3)
language packs  en, es (+ variety tables: es-MX first): numbers, clock, days, spelling -> phones, word tables, answer templates
core            capture, end of speech, engines, language decision, request shape, reader grammar, actions, responder, loader, timing
```

Rule of dependency: a layer may read only layers below it in this list; nothing below "city pack" may contain a
proper noun of a place. A test enforces it (B2).

### 2.2 Layout

```
tiny-speech/
  core/
    request.js      request shape, the permitted actions
    reader/         time, count, price, list/one, near; topic matching driven by tables; name matching by sound
    answer/         open-now logic, distance, ranking; takes tz, centre, meal hours from the pack
    respond.js      sentences tagged {lang, text, say?}; only speakable words go to the voice
    lang-decide.js  two-way decision: recognizer scores + prior + text evidence (names masked)
    pack/           schema, loader (hash check, cache, swap), name index
  lang/
    en/             numbers, clock, days, word tables, templates
    es/             the same, plus spelling -> phones rules
    es/mx.json      variety table: ahorita, almuerzo = late breakfast, "cuarto para las nueve", x and tl readings
  domains/
    places/         topic tables (cuisines, features, music genres), answer templates
    news/           feed search, read, next, stop; hand-off to a server as one action
  packs/
    src/mx-sma/     authored data, with dates and sources
    dist/           built packs (content-addressed)
  tools/            build-pack, validate, sizes, golden-test runner
  browser/          listen, worklet, workers, player, check page   (v0, built)
  engines/          AsrEngine: transformers.js Whisper (today); VoiceEngine: tiny-voca ONNX (today), tiny-voca plain JS, device voice
  test/
```

### 2.3 Interfaces (replacing the list in section 4)

- `AsrEngine.transcribe(audio, {lang|null, hint}) -> {text, lang, langScores:{en,es}, alternatives?, phones?, ms}`.
  `hint` is prepared by the pack, already within the engine's budget. Streaming (`partial`) stays optional.
- `decideLanguage({langScores, text, maskedText, prior, override}) -> {lang, margin, why}`. Pure function, tested on text.
- `Reader.read(text, now, pack) -> request`. Language-blind: reads English and Spanish words in one pass.
- `Domain.answer(request, pack, lang) -> {sentences:[{lang, text, say?}], cards, effects}`.
- `VoiceEngine.speak(sentences) / stop()`, with `phoneset` (for example `"dizione-en-1"`) so text-to-phones knows
  which sounds it may use. Which engine speaks which language is configuration: today EN = tiny-voca, ES = device.
- `Pack.load(cityId) -> pack`, `Pack.list() -> [{id, names, centre}]` (section 3).

## 3. Scaling to other cities

### 3.1 What is city-specific and where it goes

| Thing | Today | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Places, coordinates, kinds, prices, descriptions | `data.js` `PLACES`, `INFO`; `facts.js` | pack `places` |
| Opening hours | `data.js` `HOURS` **and** `hours.json` (disagreeing) | pack `live` (one source, fast-changing file) |
| Time zone | `America/Mexico_City` in `ask.js` | pack manifest `tz` (IANA name) |
| Centre, "near me" fallback, walking speed, what "Centro" means | constants in `ask.js`, regex in `read.js` | manifest `centre`, `farM`, `walkMPerMin`; `areas` with `role: "centre"` |
| Neighbourhoods | regexes in `parse()` | pack `areas` (names, aliases, point + radius or polygon) |
| Sights and landmarks ("near the Parroquia") | places with `cat: sights` + aliases in code | pack `places` with `kind: "sight"` |
| Name aliases, exact-only names, names that are ordinary words | tables in `read.js` | pack `places[].names`; flags computed by the builder |
| Curated lists (Susan York's picks) | `q.susan`, `susan.js` | pack `collections` (generic: id, spoken names, place ids, how to say it) |
| Local dishes and words | `DISHES`, `DISH_WORDS`, `TOPICS` in `read.js` | common ones: `domains/places` tables; local ones: pack `lexicon` |
| Meal hours ("lunch" = 13:00–16:00) | `MEALS` passed to the reader | variety table default, pack may override |
| Music venues, genres, nights | `live_music` feature on 3 places | pack `places[].music` |
| Pronunciation of names | `spanish.js` rules + `BY_HAND`, `ENGLISH` | rules in `lang/es`; exceptions in pack (`say`, `lang` per name) |
| Recognizer hint | built in `inline.js` | built per pack and language by the builder, within a token budget |
| Ears' dictionary and language model (future) | — | built in the browser from the pack's names and words; never trained per city |
| Map geometry (1.25 MB) | `data.js` `BASE` | a separate map pack; the voice never loads it |

### 3.2 Language variety

Three levels, so that a new place does not fork the language:

1. **Language** (`lang/es`): numbers, clock, weekdays, spelling → phones, the common word tables.
2. **Variety** (`lang/es/mx.json`, later `co`, `ar`, `es`): a table of differences only. Words (ahorita, al rato,
   mande, almuerzo), time idioms ("cuarto para las nueve" against "nueve menos cuarto"), verb forms worth
   reading (voseo: "tenés", "querés"; vosotros: "tenéis"), sound choices for the voice (seseo or distinción,
   yeísmo, sh-like ll in Rioplatense), meal hours, currency words.
3. **City** (pack `lexicon`): local words and name readings. Indigenous-origin names are the main case in
   Mexico: x is /s/ in Xochimilco, /h/ in Oaxaca, /ʃ/ in Xola; "tl", "tz", "hu" + vowel. Rules cannot choose
   between these; the pack carries the reading for each such name, and the builder lists every name that contains
   a pattern on a "needs a reading" list for the regional variety.

Design rule: **understand the union, speak the local variety.** Visitors come from everywhere, so the reader
accepts every variety's words at once (it is tables, the cost is kilobytes). The responder and the voice use
the pack's variety. English is treated the same way later (en-US, en-GB words for "bill/check", "chips/fries").

A third language (French, Portuguese, German visitors) is a language pack plus a voice that has its phones. The
contract a language pack must meet: `fold`, numbers both ways, clock both ways, weekdays, word tables for the
request's fields, answer templates, spelling → phones for a named phoneset. The request shape does not change.

Packs store names as **spelling + language tag + optional reading in IPA**, not as phones of one voice model.
The core turns them into the current voice's phoneset at load (cached). A new voice model then needs no pack
rebuild, and a pack does not pin a voice version.

```json
               "readings": {"jacques": {"ipa": "ʒak", "lang": "fr"}}, "english": ["rooftop", "inside", "geek"] },
  "built": { "nameIndex": "…", "hints": {"en": "Centro, Jardín, …", "es": "…"}, "hintTokens": {"en": 214, "es": 209},
             "collisions": [["13", "74", 0.86]] } }
```

`live.json`: `{ "hours": {"60": {"h": ["12:00–24:00", …7 entries, Monday first…], "checked": "2026-11-05"}}, "closed": {"2026-12-25": ["60"]}, "music": {…} }`.
Days are always 0–6, Monday = 0, as MapSMA's hours are; ranges may pass midnight.

**Size budget.** Measured: San Miguel's 96 places with info, hours and facts are 58.5 KB, 14.9 KB gzipped, about
155 gzipped bytes a place. With names, areas and built indexes, budget 300 gzipped bytes a place:

| City size | Places | Budget (gzipped) |
|---|---|---|
| Small (San Miguel, Guanajuato) | up to 300 | 100 KB |
| Medium (Querétaro, Oaxaca, Mérida) | up to 1,500 | 500 KB |
| Large (Mexico City, Guadalajara) | more | split into zones, each within the medium budget; the manifest lists zones with centre and radius; the loader takes the zone the question or the phone's position names, and a city-wide index of names only (about 30 bytes a place) answers "is there a place called…" |

The builder fails the build above the budget. Name matching in `read.js` compares every question window with every
name key by edit distance: fine at 96 places, **[unverified]** at 5,000; plan step B4 measures it and adds an
index (first sounds + consonant skeleton) if needed.

### 3.4 Build pipeline and validation

```
packs/src/<city>/  places.json  areas.json  hours.json  music.json  collections.json  lexicon.json  city.json
        │  tools/build-pack.mjs  (Node, no network)
        ▼
validate → derive → size check → write dist + report.md
```

Validation (each one a test that can fail the build):
- Schema; unique ids; coordinates inside `bbox`; `tz` accepted by `Intl.DateTimeFormat`; hours parse; every
  place has hours or an explicit "unknown"; `checked` dates present and not older than a set age (warn).
- Every name and alias turned into a sound key; **collisions** between places listed (two names closer than the
  matching threshold); names that are ordinary words in the reader's tables ("The Restaurant", "Inside", "Quince")
  flagged `exact` automatically. This replaces the hand-kept `EXACT_NAME` and `NO_SHORT`.
- Every name gets phones for the voice's phoneset; names matching "needs a reading" patterns without one are listed.
- Recognizer hint built by rank within the token budget; the report says which names did not fit.
- A per-city question file (`packs/src/<city>/questions.jsonl`: text, language, expected request or place id) runs
  through the reader and asker with a fixed clock in the city's time zone.
- Optional, needs the models: speak each name with the voice, transcribe it with the recognizer, check the
  reader still finds the place. A cheap, repeatable "can this name be heard" test for every new city.

Where a build-time LLM helps (never at run time): proposing aliases and likely mis-hearings per name, proposing
`what` lines in both languages, writing the question file. Its output is committed as data and reviewed as a diff.

Sources for a new city (OpenStreetMap for names and positions, the owner's or a partner's visits for facts and
hours) and their licences are an owner matter; the manifest carries `credits`.

### 3.5 Versions, cache, loading

- Version = build date + first six hex digits of the content hash. Versioned files are immutable and can be
  cached for a year; only `index.json` is fetched fresh (small, `no-cache`).
- The loader: read `index.json` (network, else the stored copy) → manifest → files; check each file's SHA-256
  with `crypto.subtle`; store in Cache Storage under `<prefix>-pack-<city>`; switch to the new version only when
  every file has checked out; keep the previous version until then. Offline, the last good pack answers, and
  the answer can say how old the hours are.
- `navigator.storage.persist()` as today. Keep at most three cities stored (least recently used goes first);
  engines are shared by all cities and are never evicted for a pack.
- Core and pack agree through `schema` and the manifest's `core` range; a pack the core cannot read is refused
  with a plain message, not half-loaded.
- Cache names come from the app config (`cachePrefix`), not from a `SITES` table or host names in the workers.

### 3.6 One site per city, or many cities in one

Both, with the same code; the app config decides:

```js
tinySpeech.start({ domains: ["places"], packs: "/packs/index.json", city: "mx-sma",        // one site, one city
                   cachePrefix: "mapsma", limits: {recMaxMs: 8000} });
tinySpeech.start({ domains: ["places"], packs: "/packs/index.json", city: "auto" });        // one app, many cities
```

`city: "auto"` chooses, in this order: the city in the address (`/c/mx-sma`), the last city used, the nearest
city centre if location permission was already given, else a short list to tap. It never asks for location just
to pick a city. "Switch to Querétaro" / "cámbiate a Querétaro" is a core action (`setCity`), matched by sound
against `index.json`'s `say` names. One pack is active at a time; a question about another city is answered with
an offer to switch, not with a search across packs. Saved places and history are keyed `city:id`.

### 3.7 What must never be city-specific

- Capture, end of speech, engines and every model file (voice, ears): one acoustic model for all cities.
- The request shape and the list of permitted actions.
- The reader's grammar: time, counts, price, near, list or one, "more", days.
- Language and variety packs.
- Answer templates and the rule that only speakable words reach the voice.
- The loader, cache and version logic; the privacy behaviour; the timing panel; the test harness.
- Code paths: no `if (city === …)`. A test greps `core/`, `lang/`, `domains/`, `browser/`, `engines/` for a list
  of proper nouns taken from every pack and fails on a hit.

## 5. Questions for the owner

1. **Which city next, and of what kind?** A small Mexican city (same variety, same time zone) needs only a pack.
   Mexico City needs zones. Another country needs a variety table and, for the voice, other sounds. This sets
   the order of work after B3.
2. **One site per city, or one app with a city switch?** The design allows both; the default decides the loader's
   first screen, the addresses, and whether saved places cross cities.
3. **Spanish answers until the bilingual voice exists:** keep the phone's own Mexican voice (my recommendation), or
   switch now to tiny-voca with an American accent as the proposal's step 2 says?
4. **Who keeps a city's data fresh, and how fresh must music be?** Recurring weekly slots with a checked date can
   be kept by one person per city. Band names and one-off dates need a feed or a partner per city; without one
   I would leave them out.
5. **Is there a GPU budget and patience for the ears, or should the plan assume Whisper tiny stays for a year?**
   If it stays, the next size win after the voice is one multilingual Whisper for both languages and self-hosted
   engine files, and the ears become a side project with a gate.
